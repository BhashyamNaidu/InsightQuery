# Domestic Violence: Definitions and Resources

As described in the arrest/domestic flag definitions document, Illinois law defines
"domestic" relationships broadly under the Illinois Domestic Violence Act of 1986 —
covering spouses and former spouses, family members, current or former household members,
co-parents, current or former dating partners, and people with disabilities and their
caregivers. This is why the `domestic` flag in crime data appears across many offense
types, not only categories commonly associated with intimate partner violence.

For general public awareness (not case-specific guidance), nationally available resources
include the National Domestic Violence Hotline (1-800-799-7233, available 24/7, with chat
and text options), which provides confidential support regardless of the caller's location
and can help connect callers to local Illinois-specific resources and shelters. The
Illinois Coalition Against Domestic Violence maintains a statewide directory of local
domestic violence service agencies offering shelter, legal advocacy, and counseling.

From an analytics-platform perspective, two points are worth flagging explicitly:

- **Under-reporting is a well-documented, persistent limitation for domestic-related
  offenses specifically.** Victims often have an ongoing relationship with the offender,
  which is associated with lower reporting rates than for crimes involving strangers.
  Reported counts for domestic-flagged incidents should be understood as a floor, not a
  complete count, of actual incidence.
- **This system does not provide, and should never be used as a substitute for, direct
  crisis support or legal advice.** Any question that appears to be seeking help with an
  active safety situation rather than requesting aggregate historical statistics should be
  answered by pointing to the resources above rather than by a statistical answer.
