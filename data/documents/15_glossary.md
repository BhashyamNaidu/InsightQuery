# Glossary of Terms

**Beat** — The smallest police patrol geography, typically a few square blocks to a few
square miles; the unit a single patrol assignment covers during a shift. Multiple beats
compose a district.

**Block (address)** — The reported location of an incident, generalized to the nearest
block (e.g., "031XX N HALSTED ST") rather than an exact address, for privacy reasons.

**Community area** — One of 76 fixed geographic regions defined for Chicago in the 1920s
for social science and planning research; boundaries are stable over time and do not
correspond to police districts or wards.

**District** — One of 22 Chicago Police Department patrol commands, each covering multiple
beats and commanded by a district commander.

**Domestic (flag)** — Indicates the incident was classified under the Illinois Domestic
Violence Act's broad definition of a domestic relationship; applies across many offense
types, not only violent crime.

**FBI code** — A two-digit code mapping a local IUCR offense code to the corresponding
national FBI/UCR offense category, used for (imperfect) cross-jurisdiction comparison.

**Index crime** — One of eight offense types (homicide, criminal sexual assault, robbery,
aggravated assault/battery, burglary, theft, motor vehicle theft, arson) designated since
1930 as the standardized national crime-rate benchmark; also called a "Part I" offense.

**IUCR code** — Illinois Uniform Crime Reporting code; a four-digit classification code
assigned by Chicago Police to every reported incident, mapping to a primary type and
secondary description.

**Primary type** — The broad offense category (e.g., THEFT, BATTERY, ASSAULT) an IUCR code
maps to; the level most crime statistics are grouped and reported at.

**Secondary description** — A more granular description of an offense within its primary
type (e.g., "RETAIL THEFT" within THEFT).

**Ward** — One of 50 Chicago City Council political/electoral districts; boundaries are
redrawn periodically after each decennial census and do not align with police districts or
community areas.
