# Chicago Police Department District Structure

The Chicago Police Department divides the city into 22 police districts, each commanded
by a district commander and responsible for patrol, response, and community engagement
within its geographic boundary. Districts are numbered (with some historical gaps from
past consolidations) and each has an official name typically drawn from a neighborhood or
landmark within its boundary — for example District 18 is "Near North" and District 19 is
"Town Hall."

Districts are further subdivided into **beats**, the smallest geographic patrol unit,
typically covering a few square blocks to a few square miles depending on population
density and call volume. A beat is the unit an individual patrol car or team is assigned
to during a shift. Multiple beats make up a district; each crime record includes both its
beat and its parent district.

Districts do not align one-to-one with **wards** (Chicago's 50 city council political
districts) or **community areas** (76 fixed, historically defined planning regions used by
the city and academic researchers since the 1920s). All three geographies — district,
ward, community area — describe overlapping but distinct boundaries, and a single
neighborhood can span parts of several districts, wards, or community areas. When
comparing crime statistics geographically, it matters which of these three units is being
used, since their boundaries were drawn for different purposes (policing operations,
political representation, and urban planning research, respectively) and do not nest
inside one another cleanly.

The Headquarters "district" that occasionally appears in supporting police datasets is an
administrative entry, not a patrol district, and is excluded from district-level crime
comparisons.
