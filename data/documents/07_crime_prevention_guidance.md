# General Crime Prevention Guidance for Residents

Municipal police departments, including Chicago's, commonly publish similar baseline
guidance for residents seeking to reduce their risk of property and personal crime. These
recommendations are general safety practice, not incident-specific advice, and do not
substitute for direct police guidance about a specific location or threat.

**Property crime:** Lock vehicles and remove visible valuables, install exterior lighting
at entry points, use a monitored alarm system or visible camera signage where feasible, and
avoid leaving packages or spare keys in obvious locations. Reporting suspicious activity
promptly — rather than only after a crime occurs — is one of the most consistently cited
factors in resolving property crime patterns, since it gives responding officers
contemporaneous information rather than delayed and less detailed reports.

**Personal safety:** Stay aware of surroundings, especially at night; travel in groups
where practical; keep phones charged and share location with trusted contacts on unfamiliar
routes; and know the location of the nearest well-lit, populated area to a planned route.

**Community-level prevention:** Participating in local CAPS beat meetings (see the CAPS
document) gives residents a direct channel to flag recurring problem locations — a
vacant building attracting break-ins, poor lighting at a specific intersection — that
often respond better to environmental or code-enforcement fixes than to increased patrol
alone.

**Reporting non-emergencies:** Non-emergency incidents can be reported through the city's
311 system or the police non-emergency line, reserving 911 for situations involving an
immediate threat to safety. Accurate, prompt reporting is also what keeps the underlying
open dataset a more complete and useful public record.
