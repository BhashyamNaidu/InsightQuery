# CAPS: Chicago Alternative Policing Strategy

The Chicago Alternative Policing Strategy (CAPS) is the city's long-running community
policing program, launched in 1993, built on the premise that reducing crime durably
requires collaboration between police, residents, and other city agencies rather than
enforcement alone. CAPS organizes regular beat community meetings — open forums, typically
monthly, tied to a specific police beat — where residents and beat officers discuss local
problems, share information, and set priorities together.

CAPS rests on four core strategies: community engagement through beat meetings and
district advisory committees; targeted problem solving that looks at recurring conditions
behind repeat incidents at a location rather than only responding call-by-call;
coordination with other city departments (streets and sanitation, building code
enforcement, mental health resources) since many quality-of-life problems residents raise
aren't purely police matters; and use of crime data and mapping to focus resources where
patterns indicate they're needed.

For an analytics platform, the practical relevance of CAPS is interpretive: a spike in
reported incidents in a given beat is not always a sign of worsening safety — it can also
reflect increased reporting following a CAPS engagement effort, a new problem-solving
initiative that surfaces previously unreported issues, or a change in patrol emphasis.
Conversely, a decline can reflect either genuinely fewer incidents or reduced reporting.
Any single-metric interpretation of a beat-level trend should be treated as a starting
hypothesis for investigation, not a conclusion, without corroborating context.
