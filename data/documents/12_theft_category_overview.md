# Theft Categories and Subtypes

THEFT is consistently the highest-volume primary crime type in Chicago's reported
incident data, which makes understanding its internal structure important for any
category-level analysis. Theft in the IUCR schema is a single primary type spanning
several distinct secondary descriptions that behave quite differently and should rarely be
aggregated without acknowledging the mix:

- **Retail theft** covers shoplifting and organized retail theft from commercial
  establishments, and tends to concentrate geographically around commercial corridors and
  shopping districts rather than being evenly distributed residentially.
- **Pocket-picking and purse-snatching** are theft-from-person subtypes, concentrated in
  dense pedestrian areas (transit stations, event venues, downtown) and, unlike most theft,
  can involve direct contact with the victim without qualifying as robbery (which legally
  requires force or threat of force).
- **Theft from a building** covers unauthorized taking of property from inside a structure
  the offender had lawful or unforced access to (distinct from burglary, which involves
  unlawful entry).
- **Theft of/from a motor vehicle** covers both stolen vehicles' contents and, when the
  vehicle itself is taken, overlaps conceptually with the separate MOTOR VEHICLE THEFT
  primary type, which is classified separately in Chicago's schema despite the similar name.
- **Dollar-value-banded theft** ("$500 and under" vs. "over $500") reflects Illinois
  statutory thresholds that determine whether an offense is charged as a misdemeanor or a
  felony, not a meaningful behavioral distinction on its own.

Because these subtypes have different victims, different locations, and different
prevention strategies, a "theft is up" headline finding is materially more useful when
broken down by secondary description than reported as a single aggregate — a rise driven by
retail theft downtown implies a very different response than a rise driven by
theft-from-motor-vehicle in residential areas.
